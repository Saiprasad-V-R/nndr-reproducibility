\caption{Recovery of an embedded response plane against a broad singular-value background.
The full stable operator is $F=O\,\mathrm{diag}(B_R,D_\beta)O^\top$,
with $D_\beta=\mathrm{diag}(0.74 j^{-\beta})$ and
$\beta\in\{0.25,0.75,1.25,1.75,2.25\}$.
The coupling of the planted two-dimensional block is recalibrated to the corrected
continuous-time rate-based reference ratios
$R_{A,\star}\in\{1,3,10,30\}$, while its eigenvalues remain
$0.945$ and $0.455$. These are new coupling strengths, not relabeled historical
matrices. The independently specified reference plane is $Q_\star=O_{:,1:2}$.
The experiment uses $N=80$, eight independent seeds per setting, $M=18$ independent
training trajectories of 220 one-step transitions, Gaussian innovation standard
deviation $0.035$, and absolute ridge regularization $10^{-4}$.
(a) Full-operator singular spectra normalized by $\sigma_1(F)$ at
$R_{A,\star}=3$. (b) Largest principal-angle error between the extracted and
planted planes, with means and standard errors across all 4 strengths and 8
seeds at each exponent. All 320 method evaluations enter (b).
(c) Mean absolute ratio-recovery error
$\mathcal{E}_{R_A}=\left|\log_{10}(\widehat R_A/R_{A,\star})\right|$
for admissible reduced matrices only, with standard errors.
(d) Inferred $\widehat R_A$ versus independently imposed $R_{A,\star}$;
the dashed line indicates equality. Circles denote M2 and triangles M3.
In the companion version, faint dotted curves in (c) show the corresponding
$\mathcal{E}_{R_\sigma}=|\log_{10}(\widehat R_\sigma/R_{\sigma,\star})|$,
and faint open markers in (d) show $\widehat R_\sigma$ against its own
matching reference $R_{\sigma,\star}$, not against $R_{A,\star}$.
$R_\sigma$ is defined for 289/320 fitted maps, compared with 288/320 for $R_A$.
The $R_A$ ratio is inadmissible in 32/160 M2 fits, all at
$R_{A,\star}=1$ (31 unstable reduced compressions and one negative-real
reduced multiplier); no values are imputed in panels (c,d).
All 160 M3 fits are admissible. The weak-amplification regime exhibits
substantially larger plane errors despite recovery at stronger couplings.}
